## A. Oceananigans — `distributed-implicit-velocity-halos-pr`

**Title:** Fill the velocity halos read by the implicit velocity steps

## What this fixes

An implicit flux boundary condition on one velocity component can read the other component next to the column — a
semi-implicit quadratic bottom drag, for one, whose coefficient is `-μ √(u² + ℑxy v²)`. Both components are stepped
explicitly before either is stepped implicitly, so on a periodic side or next to a rank edge that coefficient read a
halo still holding the value from before the explicit step, while the interior next to it held the value after it.
A distributed run then differed from the same run on one rank at every rank edge, and a periodic run at its periodic
ends.

## Changes

- `fill_halo_regions!` gains `only_connected_halos = false`: with `true`, only periodic, multi-region and
  communicated halos are filled.
- `rk_substep_velocities!` and `ab2_step_velocities!` fill those halos of `v` before the implicit step of `u`, and of
  `u` before that of `v`, when a velocity has an implicit flux boundary condition.

## Test

`test/mpi/hydrostatic_model.jl`: a periodic box with a semi-implicit quadratic bottom drag, AB2 and split RK3, serial
vs distributed. It fails on `main` (relative differences ~1e-5) and passes with the change.

## B. Oceananigans — `distributed-regular-coordinates-pr`

**Title:** Make the nodes of a regular coordinate independent of its halo and partition

## What this fixes

A regular coordinate was a range starting at the first halo face, so its nodes depended in the last bit on the halo
size, and each rank of a distributed grid built its own range from a rounded local interval. So:

- a grid copied with a wider halo (`with_halo`, which the split-explicit free surface uses in connected directions)
  had nodes and metrics a few ulps from those of the grid it came from;
- the ranks of a distributed grid had nodes and metrics a few ulps from those of the same grid on one rank.

Fields set from node positions (an interpolated initial condition, for one) then differed from the serial ones at
~1e-13 from the first step.

## Changes

- The faces of a regular coordinate are `range(c₁, c₂, length = N + 1)`, extended into the halos with the same
  reference and step; the centers lie half a step from them. The interior nodes, ends included, no longer depend on
  the halo, and the end faces are exactly `c₁` and `c₂`.
- `partition_coordinate` returns, for an interval, a `PartitionedInterval`, and each rank evaluates the nodes of the
  whole coordinate at its own indices.

## Tests

- `test/unit/grids.jl`: `with_halo` keeps the nodes of a `LatitudeLongitudeGrid`, and its end faces are the given
  bounds.
- `test/mpi/architectures.jl`: each rank's nodes, areas and spacings equal those of the grid on one rank.

Both fail on `main`.

## C. Oceananigans — `catke-surface-buoyancy-flux-halos-pr`

**Title:** Fill the halos of CATKE's averaged surface buoyancy flux after its update

## What this fixes

CATKE's diffusivities are computed one cell into the halos, where they read the averaged surface buoyancy flux
`Jᵇ`. `Jᵇ` is updated over the interior only, and its halos were never filled across a rank edge (the closure
fields are filled with `only_local_halos = true`), so the diffusivity next to a rank edge read a stale `Jᵇ` wherever
the surface flux was convective.

## Changes

`step_closure_prognostics!` fills the halos of `Jᵇ` after updating it.

## Test

`test/mpi/hydrostatic_model.jl`: convection under a surface buoyancy loss varying along `x`, serial vs distributed.
Fails on `main` (TKE off by ~3e-4 relative after 60 steps), passes with the change.

## D. Oceananigans — `oblique-radiation-rank-edges-pr`

**Title:** Read the tangential differences of ObliqueRadiation across rank edges and periodic ends

## What this fixes

`ObliqueRadiation` keeps the boundary and first-interior values of the previous iteration in arrays along the
boundary, and its tangential differences were clamped at the ends of those arrays. A rank edge, or the periodic end
of a boundary, therefore acted as an end of the boundary.

## Changes

- The previous values are kept in fields reduced normal to the boundary; their halos are filled in
  `update_boundary_condition!`.
- The tangential differences read across a connected or periodic side, and are clamped only at a bounded end.

## Test

`test/mpi/split_explicit_boundaries.jl`: a tracer leaving a periodic channel through `ObliqueRadiation` boundaries,
serial vs distributed (`Partition(4)` and `Partition(2, 2)`). Fails on `main`, passes with the change.

(The tangential-velocity part of #6091 needs the same treatment for its neighbouring normal velocities; I will add it
to #6091 once this and the implicit-halo PR are in.)

## E. NumericalEarth — `exchanger-temperature-halo`

**Title:** Fill the exchanger ocean temperature over the interface range with completed halos

## What this fixes

The air-sea fluxes are computed over `interface_kernel_parameters`, one cell into the halos, but the exchanger's
ocean temperature was filled over `:xy`, and right after the ocean step, while the halo exchange of the ocean
tracers started at the end of that step could still be in flight. Next to a rank edge the fluxes read a temperature
that was never set, or that belonged to the previous step.

## Changes

`interpolate_state!` completes the halo exchange of `T` and `S` and fills the exchanger temperature over the
interface range.

## Test

`test/test_distributed_utils.jl`: after a coupled step, the exchanger temperature equals `θ_from_Θ(S, T)` of the
ocean surface over the whole interface range, halos included (`Partition(4, 1)`, `(1, 4)`, `(2, 2)`). On `main` the
halo rows are off by the full temperature.
